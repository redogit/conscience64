# Conscience64 MMO RPG — Reality Canon

**Status:** canonical for the MMO branch.  
**Applies to:** `play/mmo/` world presentation, activity writing, plug-in examples, release review, and future game art.  
**Precedence:** when an older MMO aesthetic or content note conflicts with this file, this file wins. Historical files remain preserved as predecessor state; they are not rewritten into having always used this direction.

## System-canon relation

`RMAO_WORLD_CANON.md` controls the successor world/system target. This file still controls grounded visual and reality presentation. The successor may be violent and impossible while materials, scale, lighting, provenance, and the observation-vs-fiction boundary remain explicit.

## Core direction

The world should feel **real before it becomes impossible**.

Start from recognizable ordinary life:

- apartments, houses, storefronts, workshops, schools, parks, transit, sidewalks, parking lots, service roads and public spaces;
- brick, concrete, asphalt, glass, steel, wood, paint, dirt, water, plants, fabric, plastic and worn surfaces;
- believable weathering, reflections, shadows, practical lighting and human-scale proportions;
- people going to work, shopping, waiting, repairing, cooking, playing, talking, resting and helping one another;
- ordinary choices that are worth doing even when no quest, reward or anomaly appears.

Then let the strange material intrude:

- monsters;
- impossible geometry;
- Fuzzball;
- moving rules;
- unexpected research connections;
- black-hole and cosmic phenomena;
- unexplained places and events.

An anomaly is more powerful when the surrounding world is credible.

## Visual hierarchy

Use this order:

1. **Ordinary physical world** — believable materials, scale, light, wear and human activity.
2. **Observed natural world** — real stars, sky structure, plants, weather, water, terrain and biological cues.
3. **Scientific observation references** — astronomy and other scientific imagery with source/provenance retained.
4. **Processed scientific visualization** — clearly distinguish wavelength mapping, reconstruction and composite imagery from unaided-eye appearance.
5. **Game reconstruction** — visually inspired by real structure but explicitly fictional or illustrative.
6. **Anomaly/fantasy layer** — monsters, portals, impossible structures and other departures from reality.

Higher layers never silently claim the evidentiary status of lower layers.

## Astronomy canon

Astronomy should be based on the structure of real observations rather than generic space wallpaper.

Reference anchors:

- Event Horizon Telescope M87* image: a bright emission ring around a central black-hole shadow, reconstructed from radio observations.
- Event Horizon Telescope Sagittarius A* image: the supermassive black hole at the center of the Milky Way.
- Milky Way center imagery: dense stars, gas, dust and complex structure rather than empty black space.
- James Webb Space Telescope imagery: infrared observations mapped into visible colors; published color is scientifically processed, not a literal unaided-eye view.

Official reference pages:

- https://eventhorizontelescope.org/press-release-april-10-2019-astronomers-capture-first-image-black-hole
- https://eventhorizontelescope.org/blog/astronomers-reveal-first-image-black-hole-heart-our-galaxy
- https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/science-overview/science-explainers/how-are-webbs-full-color-images-made/

Required boundary:

`OBSERVATION != PROCESSED_SCIENCE_IMAGE != GAME_RECONSTRUCTION`

Any in-game astronomical reconstruction must be labeled when a player could reasonably mistake it for telescope data.

## Color and lighting

The old all-red presentation is no longer the world baseline.

- Red remains a **Conscience64 identity/accent color**: signs, wayfinding, interface focus, occasional architecture, event lighting and anomalies.
- The world itself uses plausible material colors and environmental lighting.
- Night scenes should contain practical light sources: windows, streetlights, vehicles, signs, moonlight, work lights and interior spill.
- Wet surfaces should reflect nearby sources rather than glow independently.
- Cosmic scenes should preserve black levels and sparse/dense structure appropriate to the scene instead of filling every region with saturated color.

## People and everyday life

NPCs and players are not decorative population counts.

World spaces should visibly support things people actually do:

- travel;
- work;
- eat;
- repair;
- build;
- learn;
- shop;
- exercise;
- play;
- socialize;
- rest;
- care for others;
- create things;
- observe the world.

Gameplay may arise from these ordinary activities before becoming extraordinary.

## Arcade Forge / plug-in rule

Mini-games should normally have a **grounding anchor** before their absurd twist.

Preferred recipe:

`real place or object + understandable activity + optional strange complication`

Examples:

- repair a loose shelf in a workshop while one tool keeps moving;
- catch a bus on time while the route map changes one stop;
- help recover a dropped market display item after unusual wind;
- identify a real star-field pattern before Fuzzball adds one impossible point;
- carry supplies through a normal alley that temporarily refuses Euclidean distance.

The absurd layer is optional. A fully ordinary mini-game is valid.

## Reality and privacy boundaries

Do not fake or imply access to facts the game does not have.

- Fictional game weather/time must be labeled as game state when needed.
- Do not imply the player is physically located at the fictional street or district.
- Do not require real geolocation for ordinary play.
- Do not present generated people as real identifiable people.
- Do not present a game reconstruction as a photograph, telescope exposure or scientific measurement.
- Do not turn Conscience64 retrieval into external evidence.

## Release invariant

For `RIPPING MANY ARMS OFF 1.0`, every major world surface should answer four questions:

1. What ordinary place or activity anchors this scene?
2. Which materials/light/physical cues make it believable?
3. What, if anything, is anomalous?
4. Is the boundary between observed reality, scientific processing and game fiction clear?

A scene that cannot answer these questions should be revised before release unless abstraction is itself necessary to the activity.
