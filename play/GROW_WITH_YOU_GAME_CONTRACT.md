# Grow-With-You Game Contract

This is the public design contract for Conscience64 games intended to remain enjoyable as a player grows.

## Purpose

Build games a child can enjoy now and keep discovering later. Research, mathematics, language, science, accessibility work, and technical systems are supporting materials for play; they are not prerequisites for being welcome in the world.

The player does not need credentials, prior knowledge, or a particular way of learning.

The project may also become a sustainable source of income for the people who build, maintain, support, and care for it. Sustainability must come from clear value, not from exploiting attention, fear, chance, or children.

## Core rule

**Add depth without taking away agency.**

A later layer should create new possibilities without making the earlier game invalid. A player who wants to explore, collect, build, dance, race, make patterns, hear stories, or simply wander should still be able to do that after deeper systems exist.

## Play layers

These are readiness layers, not age labels. The game should not need to collect a child's age to choose them.

1. **Explore** — move, look, listen, discover, collect, follow friendly signals.
2. **Make** — build, decorate, draw, arrange, grow, repair, invent simple things.
3. **Story** — characters, mysteries, choices, humor, consequences, recurring places.
4. **Systems** — crafting, ecology, trade without gambling pressure, logistics, cooperation, cause and effect.
5. **Discovery** — astronomy, nature, language, music, mathematics, engineering and other real knowledge clearly separated from fiction.
6. **Deep world** — difficult puzzles, long projects, strategy, creation tools, research-inspired mysteries and cooperative challenges.

Moving deeper is optional. Earlier layers remain playable.

## Safety and dignity

- Child-safe default surface.
- No sexual or adult material in child-facing game content.
- No gambling, wagers, purchasable random-chance rewards, or loot-box pressure.
- No requirement to spend money to make ordinary progress.
- No manipulative streaks, fear-of-missing-out timers, or punishment for leaving the game.
- No child age profiling required for ordinary play.
- Minimize collection of personal information; local-first play is preferred where practical.
- Multiplayer, communication, identity and user-generated-content features remain separately gated until their child-safety, moderation, privacy and security requirements are actually implemented.
- Difficulty can increase; humiliation should not be a game mechanic.
- Failure can teach, reveal, transform or become part of the story without becoming a judgment about the player.

## Sustainable money without predatory design

The game can make money. The constraint is how.

Preferred routes include:

- straightforward ownership or permanent-access purchases;
- optional creator/pro tools for people who want deeper building capabilities;
- family or supporter packs that add convenience or creation tools rather than power over other players;
- donations and voluntary support;
- clearly priced expansions or substantial new worlds;
- merchandising or physical extras only when fulfillment and rights are actually ready.

Avoid:

- gambling-like monetization;
- paid random rewards;
- manipulative scarcity or fear-of-missing-out;
- pay-to-win advantages;
- child-targeted pressure to purchase;
- hidden subscriptions;
- selling player conversations or behavioral data to advertisers.

The business should support the family, friends, collaborators, maintainers, artists, and infrastructure around the project without making exploitation part of the game loop.

## Growing without homogenizing

Different players should be able to enjoy the same world differently. Exploration, building, stories, puzzles, science, music and social cooperation are parallel paths, not a single ladder everyone must climb.

The design target is:

`EARLIER PLAY REMAINS VALID + NEW OPTIONAL DEPTH = GROWTH`

A player should be able to return years later and recognize the places, characters, creations and history that mattered before while finding genuinely new things to do.

## Reality and imagination

Fiction is allowed to be wild. Real science, history, geography or other factual material must remain distinguishable from fictional reconstruction.

`GAME FICTION != OBSERVATION != ESTABLISHED EVIDENCE`

The point is not to turn play into school. The point is to let curiosity have somewhere honest to go when the player asks, "Is that real?"

## Accessibility

Growth includes more ways to participate, not fewer. Keyboard, pointer, touch, reduced-motion, forced-colors, readable text, audio alternatives and assistive-technology compatibility should be treated as game capabilities. Automated checks are not a substitute for testing with actual players and assistive technologies.

## Long horizon

The world should preserve lineage. Major changes should retain enough history, save compatibility or migration information, and recognizable structure that the game feels like an evolving place rather than a replacement that erased the player's past.

The goal is not an infinitely complicated game.

The goal is a world worth coming back to — and a project worth sustaining.
